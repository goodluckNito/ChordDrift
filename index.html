Chord Drift

Explore chord progressions as voice-led glides: pick a key and scale, write or generate chords, and hear every voice move. The seed decides whatever you leave open.

Key & harmony

Drums are off, so full parts only open up the sub. Turn on Drums to hear them in the ! parts.

Song form
A B C
Parts (names are letters). A name that comes back plays the same chords again.
A4 B16
Length in bars (default 8). Chords change on the chord rhythm, or once a bar without one.
B!8
A full part: the sub bass opens up, the drums play and the chop (if on) kicks in. Parts without ! are soft: no drums, almost no sub.
B!8[kh] A8[h]
Pick the drums for a part: k(ick) s(nare) h(at) c(rash) r(ide). [] means none. Without brackets, full parts get every drum and soft parts none.
B8{E,dorian} C!8{jazz}
Set a part's key, scale and/or style. A repeat in another key is transposed; one in another scale or style is a new variation with its own chords.
up4{climb}
Climb: the bass walks up from the tonic to the dominant, a step per chord, and the next part resolves it.
R2{release}
Release: one long suspended dominant (V7sus4) for the whole part, then home to the tonic.
A8+2 B!4-3
Move the part up or down that many semitones from the key.
A | B
Collapse: every voice converges on a unison (the next part's tonic, or high on its dominant), holds, then blooms out into the next part.
A > B
Resolve straight from one part into the next.

With Loop on, the voices glide back to the starting unison after the ending, so any ending loops cleanly.

Rhythm

Strikes re-attack the current chord at the tempo; chords still change on their own timing (Chord rhythm, or free timing). Rests skip a strike.

Rhythm notation

Whole, half, quarter, eighth, sixteenth. Numbers work too: 1 2 4 8 16 32.
q. h..
Dotted (×1.5) and double-dotted (×1.75).
et et et ht 8t
Triplets (×⅔) — three eighth-triplets fill one beat.
h+e
Tie: one note lasting a half plus an eighth.
rq r8
Rests. In a chord rhythm a rest just holds the chord longer.
e*4 (q e e)*2
Repeat a note or a group. Patterns loop; | and , are ignored.

A beat is a quarter note at the tempo. Chord rhythms start when the bloom lands; each chord's glide is fitted inside its slot.

One character per 16th note, looping: x play, . cut, - half volume, < reverse (a run plays that stretch backwards), = repeat (a run of n replays the n steps before it), ~ tape stop (slows to a stop), ? random (the seed decides each bar).
